# 1. Executive Summary

FleetOps MZ is a B2B SaaS platform designed to help transport, logistics and fleet-intensive companies manage their vehicles, drivers, trips, operational expenses and fleet performance through a centralized digital platform.

The product is being developed for the operational realities of Mozambique, with an initial commercial focus on companies operating around Beira and the country's major transport and logistics corridors.

The commercial strategy is to position FleetOps MZ as a practical operational management solution that helps companies:

- Improve visibility over fleet operations.
- Centralize transport and trip information.
- Monitor fuel and operational expenses.
- Improve vehicle and driver coordination.
- Understand the costs associated with transport operations.
- Support better operational and financial decisions.

The immediate objective is not mass-market adoption. It is to acquire a small number of qualified business customers, validate the product with real operations and establish a repeatable sales process.

# 7. Problems to Validate During Customer Research

The following are potential pain points to investigate.

They must not be assumed to apply to every prospect.

## Operational management

- Vehicle information is distributed across spreadsheets and documents.
- Trip information is difficult to consolidate.
- Managers depend on manual communication to obtain operational updates.
- Vehicle and driver assignments are not consistently documented.

## Fuel management

- Fuel purchases are difficult to reconcile with vehicle and trip records.
- Fuel expenditure is not consistently analyzed.
- Managers lack structured consumption comparisons.
- Receipts and purchase records are difficult to consolidate.

Important technical distinction:

Fuel purchased is not necessarily equal to fuel consumed.

Reliable consumption analysis requires suitable odometer readings, tank-level information, telematics or other measurement methods and correct period reconciliation.

GPS location alone does not establish litres consumed.

## Maintenance

- Maintenance records are fragmented.
- Maintenance costs are difficult to consolidate.
- Vehicle service history is incomplete.
- Preventive maintenance planning is difficult.

## Financial control

- Trip costs are difficult to calculate.
- Revenue and direct operating expenses are not consistently associated with individual trips.
- Managers have limited visibility over trip contribution margins.
- Management reporting requires manual work.

Trip contribution margin must not be described as net profit unless all relevant costs have been included.

## Driver management

- Driver information is fragmented.
- Driver and vehicle assignments are difficult to track.
- Operational records depend on manual communication.
- Driver documents and operational information are not centralized.

# 10. Go-to-Market Strategy

## 10.1 Phase 1 — Market validation

**Objective:** Identify real customer needs and validate commercial fit.

Actions:

1. Identify 15–20 target companies.
2. Prioritize transport and logistics businesses in Beira.
3. Identify relevant business contacts.
4. Conduct discovery conversations.
5. Understand existing operational processes.
6. Document current systems and alternatives.
7. Identify willingness to test a new platform.
8. Refine the commercial proposition.

Expected outcome:

A validated list of target companies and a clearer understanding of the most important customer problems.

## 10.2 Phase 2 — Product demonstrations

**Objective:** Convert market interest into qualified opportunities.

Actions:

- Prepare a professional product demonstration.
- Use realistic sample company data.
- Demonstrate relevant workflows.
- Focus on business outcomes.
- Ask prospects to explain their current processes.
- Record questions, objections and requested improvements.
- Prepare tailored proposals.

Recommended demonstration scenarios:

1. Register a vehicle.
2. Register and assign a driver.
3. Create and manage a trip.
4. Register fuel purchases.
5. Record trip expenses.
6. Review vehicle and trip information.
7. Analyze trip costs and contribution margin.

Only demonstrate workflows that are functional in the current build.

## 10.3 Phase 3 — Pilot customers

**Objective:** Validate the platform using real operational workflows.

Initial target: 2–3 pilot companies.

Suggested duration: 30–45 days.

Pilot activities:

- Customer onboarding.
- Initial configuration.
- Data registration.
- User training.
- Operational workflow testing.
- Weekly feedback meetings.
- Issue tracking.
- Adoption measurement.
- Commercial review.

The pilot must define the exact modules and workflows included.

## 10.4 Phase 4 — Commercial conversion

**Objective:** Convert successful pilots into paying customers.

Actions:

- Review pilot results.
- Present validated operational benefits.
- Address outstanding issues.
- Agree on subscription plan.
- Formalize commercial terms.
- Establish onboarding and support processes.

## 10.5 Phase 5 — Growth and expansion

After validating the initial market:

- Develop customer referral programs.
- Build strategic partnerships.
- Publish real customer testimonials with permission.
- Expand prospecting to Maputo, Nacala and Tete.
- Develop vertical-specific campaigns.
- Explore regional expansion.

# 15. Lead Qualification and Sales Process

Every potential customer should pass through a structured process.

## 15.1 Lead stages

1. Discovered.
2. Identified.
3. Qualified.
4. Researched.
5. Contact identified.
6. Outreach prepared.
7. Outreach approved.
8. Contacted.
9. Responded.
10. Meeting scheduled.
11. Demonstration completed.
12. Proposal sent.
13. Pilot negotiation.
14. Pilot active.
15. Customer won.
16. Customer lost.
17. Follow-up required.

## 15.2 Qualification questions

During discovery conversations, investigate:

1. How many vehicles does the company operate?
2. What types of vehicles are used?
3. Does the company operate domestic or cross-border transport?
4. How are trips currently managed?
5. How is fuel expenditure controlled?
6. How are maintenance records maintained?
7. How are operational expenses registered?
8. How does management calculate trip costs?
9. What software or tools are currently used?
10. Who is responsible for evaluating new operational software?
11. What would make a new system valuable enough to adopt?
12. Is the company willing to participate in a pilot?

Do not treat a prospect as qualified solely because it operates trucks.

# 18. Risks and Commercial Considerations

## Product readiness

Do not onboard customers into workflows that are not sufficiently tested.

## Data security

Before using real customer data, validate:

- Supabase Row Level Security (RLS).
- Tenant data isolation.
- Role-based access.
- Authentication.
- Backups and recovery.
- Data export and deletion procedures.
- Error handling and auditability.

## GPS and integrations

Do not advertise live tracking, fuel-card integrations or telematics integrations until they have been implemented, tested and authorized.

## Financial claims

Do not promise specific savings or profitability improvements without customer evidence.

## Market competition

Investigate existing software and operational alternatives before claiming competitive superiority.

## Customer acquisition

Do not confuse social media reach with qualified B2B demand.

## Privacy and outreach

Use legitimate business contact sources, comply with applicable privacy and communication rules, and respect opt-out requests.
